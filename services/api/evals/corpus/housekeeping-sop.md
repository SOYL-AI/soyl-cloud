# Housekeeping Standard Operating Procedure

Sanchari Hotels — all properties. Effective 1 April 2026.

## 1. Morning allocation and briefing

Room allocation is issued at the 07:00 briefing and covers departures,
stay-overs, arrivals expected before 14:00, and rooms held out of order. A room
attendant works from the allocation sheet and does not re-order it without
telling the floor supervisor, because the order is set against the arrivals
list rather than by floor.

The briefing covers the occupancy for the day, the arrival and departure counts,
any guest with a stated preference or allergy, any room under maintenance, and
the previous day's inspection failures. Attendance is recorded. A member of
staff who arrives after the briefing is briefed individually by the supervisor
before starting on the floor.

Trolleys are stocked before the first room is entered and are never left
unattended in a corridor with the linen cupboard or the chemical tray exposed.
A trolley is positioned across the doorway of the room being serviced so the
doorway remains visibly occupied.

## 2. Servicing a departure room

A departure room is stripped, cleaned, replenished and inspected within
forty-five minutes. The room is not released to the front desk until the floor
supervisor has inspected it and marked it clean in the system; a room marked
clean by the attendant alone is not saleable.

Stripping begins with the removal of all linen and all used amenities, which
leave the room before cleaning begins. Bins are emptied and re-lined. Every
horizontal surface is cleaned, including the tops of door frames, the underside
of the desk, and the frame of the bed base, which are the three places most
often missed and most often reported.

The bathroom is cleaned last, from the highest surface downward, finishing with
the floor. Shower screens are dried rather than left to air. The toilet brush
and holder are cleaned as part of the fixture and not passed over.

Anything left behind by the previous guest goes to lost property under section
8 before the room is released, and never into the bin, regardless of apparent
value.

## 3. Servicing an occupied room

An occupied room is serviced once daily between 09:00 and 16:00 unless the
guest has arranged otherwise. The attendant knocks, announces the department,
waits for a count of ten, and repeats before entering. Where the guest is in
the room, the attendant asks whether it is convenient and returns later if it
is not.

Guest belongings are not moved beyond what is necessary to clean the surface
they rest on, and are returned to the position they were found in. Papers are
not stacked, clothing is not folded, and nothing is moved from the floor into a
drawer or cupboard. A guest's possessions are never handled with the intent of
tidying them.

Linen on an occupied room is changed every third night and on request. Towels
are changed daily where they are found on the floor or in the bath and left in
place where they are hung, in line with the notice displayed in the bathroom.

Where an attendant finds something in an occupied room that gives them concern
— a substantial quantity of medication, a weapon, an animal, signs of smoking,
or apparatus that could cause a fire — they leave the room and report to the
floor supervisor immediately without touching it.

## 4. Privacy requests and unserviced rooms

A room displaying a privacy request is not entered and is not knocked on. The
attendant records the time observed and moves on.

A privacy request displayed continuously beyond 15:00 is reported to the floor
supervisor, who telephones the room. Where there is no answer, the duty manager
is informed. A room that has displayed a privacy request for twenty-four
continuous hours is entered by the duty manager accompanied by a second member
of staff, in accordance with the welfare provisions of the safety standard.

A guest who declines service for the day is offered fresh towels and drinking
water at the door, and the room is flagged for a full service the following day.
A room that has declined service for three consecutive days is serviced on the
fourth regardless, and the guest is informed of this at the point of the third
refusal rather than at the door on the fourth day.

## 5. Linen handling and laundry despatch

Soiled linen is placed directly into the trolley bag and is never placed on the
floor, on furniture, or in the corridor. Linen and towels are bagged separately
from one another and separately from any item stained with blood or bodily
fluid, which is placed in a marked red bag and handled with gloves.

Counts are taken at despatch and at return, by item type, and reconciled the
same day. A discrepancy of more than two per cent is reported to the executive
housekeeper before the next despatch rather than accumulated to a monthly
figure.

Clean linen is stored in a closed cupboard, off the floor, and is not stacked
against an outside wall where condensation forms. Stock is rotated so that the
oldest clean linen is used first. Linen that is torn, permanently marked, or
thinned to the point of transparency is withdrawn from circulation and recorded
as a condemnation rather than returned to the cupboard.

Guest laundry is collected against a signed list, counted in the presence of the
guest where possible, and returned within twenty-four hours. An item damaged in
our care is settled at the depreciated replacement value, and the executive
housekeeper informs the guest before departure rather than leaving it to be
discovered.

## 6. Amenities, minibar and replenishment

Bathroom amenities are replenished to the standard set for the room category at
every service, whether or not the previous set has been used, other than
part-used bar soap and part-used bottles in an occupied room, which are left in
place until finished.

Drinking water is provided at two bottles per occupant per day at no charge and
is replenished at every service. Water is never charged to a guest account in
any category of room.

Minibar consumption is checked at every service and posted to the room account
the same day. Consumption discovered at departure that was not posted during the
stay is written off rather than raised at the desk, because a charge a guest
cannot verify is not worth the argument it produces. Minibar stock is checked
against date of expiry weekly and anything within thirty days of expiry is
withdrawn.

Stationery, laundry lists, the room directory and the safety notice are checked
at every service and replaced where marked, torn, or written on.

## 7. Deep cleaning and the rotation schedule

Every room receives a deep clean once each quarter, scheduled so that no more
than four rooms are out of service on any day. The schedule is published a
month in advance and is not moved to accommodate occupancy without the approval
of the executive housekeeper.

A deep clean covers the mattress and the mattress protector, the upholstery, the
curtains and their headings, the carpet or the floor beneath the bed, the air
conditioning grille and filter, behind and beneath all fixed furniture, the
inside of the wardrobe, the shower head and its aerator, and the silicone
sealant throughout the bathroom.

Mattresses are turned or rotated according to their type at each deep clean and
the date is recorded on the label stitched into the mattress. Curtains are
cleaned annually. Carpets are shampooed twice a year, and the room is released
only when the carpet is dry to the touch rather than after a fixed interval.

A room that fails inspection after a deep clean is re-cleaned before it returns
to sale, and the failure is recorded against the schedule so that the pattern
is visible over a quarter.

## 8. Lost and found

Any item found anywhere on the property is handed to the housekeeping desk on
the same shift, recorded with the place, date and time of finding and the name
of the finder, and stored in the locked lost property store. Items are never
kept in a locker, a trolley, or a member of staff's own possession overnight.

Items of apparent value — jewellery, electronics, documents, medication, cash,
and anything carrying a name — are logged separately and stored in the safe
rather than on the open shelf. The duty manager countersigns the entry the same
day.

We attempt to contact the guest for items of apparent value on the day of
finding, using the contact details on the reservation. Items of no apparent
value are held without contact. Postage for return is charged to the guest at
cost and never at a margin.

Items of apparent value are held for six months, and other items for three
months. Unclaimed items are offered to the finder after that period, other than
cash, documents and medication, which are surrendered to the police or
destroyed as appropriate. Nothing is disposed of without a second signature.

## 9. Public areas and cleaning frequency

Lobby floors are cleaned continuously through the day and washed after 23:00
when traffic is low. Public lavatories are checked and signed every hour between
06:00 and 23:00, and the check sheet is displayed inside the door.

Glass, brass and lift interiors are attended to twice daily. Lift tracks are
cleaned nightly, being the point where a lobby looks neglected before anything
else does. Corridors are vacuumed daily after 10:00 and never during the night.

Wet floor signage is placed before cleaning begins and removed the moment the
floor is dry, not left standing. A sign left in place when the floor is dry
teaches guests to walk past it when it matters.

Outdoor areas, the porch and the car park approach are swept before 07:00.
Ashtrays and bins in the smoking area are cleared at least four times daily.

## 10. Chemical handling and staff safety

Cleaning chemicals are used only from the dispensing station at the dilution
marked on the dispenser. Chemicals are never decanted into an unlabelled
bottle, and never into a bottle that has held anything else. A container
without a legible label is emptied and discarded rather than used.

Gloves are worn for all bathroom cleaning and for any handling of soiled linen.
Eye protection is worn when decanting concentrate. Acid-based descalers and
chlorine-based products are never used in the same room in the same session,
because the combination produces chlorine gas in an enclosed bathroom.

Safety data sheets for every product in use are held at the housekeeping desk
and in the chemical store, and every member of staff is shown where they are
during induction rather than only told that they exist.

A spillage of concentrate is contained and reported to the executive
housekeeper. A member of staff affected by a chemical is taken to the first aid
point and the incident is recorded, however minor it appears at the time.

## 11. Pest control and reporting

Any sighting of a pest is reported the same day through the housekeeping desk,
whether it is in a guest area or a service area, and whether or not it is
thought to be isolated. A single sighting is a report, not a judgement about
infestation.

The contracted pest control service attends monthly and after any report. The
service record is held by the executive housekeeper and reviewed against
sightings each quarter rather than filed.

A guest room with a confirmed sighting is taken out of service immediately, the
guest is moved to another room with an apology and no charge for the move, and
the room is not returned to sale until it has been treated and inspected. A
report of bedbugs additionally takes the two adjacent rooms and the room above
and below out of service pending inspection.

Food waste is never stored in a guest corridor or a service lobby overnight, and
service doors to the outside are kept closed rather than propped for
ventilation.

## 12. Inspection and quality standards

Every departure room is inspected before release. A minimum of five occupied
rooms per floor per day are inspected on a rotating basis so that the standard
of stay-over service is measured and not assumed.

Inspection is against the published checklist and is recorded pass or fail with
the reason. A failure is corrected before release and is recorded even when the
correction takes two minutes, because a failure that is corrected without being
recorded removes the only signal that the standard is drifting.

The executive housekeeper inspects a random sample of ten rooms each week
independently of the floor supervisors, and the general manager inspects five
rooms each month. Divergence between the supervisor pass rate and the
independent pass rate is treated as a training matter rather than a
disciplinary one in the first instance.

Guest comments concerning cleanliness are matched to the room and the attendant
who serviced it and reviewed monthly, and a pattern against one room is
investigated as a fault with the room before it is treated as a fault with the
person.
