# People and Employment Policy

Sanchari Hotels — all properties. Effective 1 April 2026. Where this policy is
less favourable than the law applying at a property, the law prevails.

## 1. Working hours, shifts and rest

The standard working week is forty-eight hours over six days. A shift is nine
hours including a one-hour break, and no member of staff works more than five
hours continuously without a break.

Shifts are published fourteen days in advance. A published shift is changed only
with the agreement of the person affected, other than in an emergency, and a
change made in an emergency is compensated with an equivalent period of rest
within the following seven days.

There is a minimum of eleven consecutive hours between the end of one shift and
the start of the next. A closing shift followed by an opening shift the
following morning breaches this and is not rostered, whatever the staffing
pressure; the department head finds another answer.

Overtime is voluntary, authorised in advance by the department head, and paid at
twice the ordinary rate. Overtime worked without authorisation is nonetheless
paid, and the failure to authorise it is a matter for the department head rather
than for the person who worked it.

## 2. Attendance, punctuality and absence

Attendance is recorded by the biometric terminal at the staff entrance at the
start and end of every shift. Recording attendance on behalf of another person
is gross misconduct for both parties.

A member of staff unable to attend telephones the department head at least four
hours before the shift begins, or as soon as they are able where that is not
possible. A message sent to a colleague is not notification.

Absence of more than two consecutive days for sickness requires a medical
certificate. Absence for sickness is not counted against the annual leave
entitlement and is not a factor in appraisal.

Persistent unexplained lateness is addressed in conversation before it is
addressed in process. Where the cause is transport, childcare or accommodation,
the department head considers a change of shift before considering a warning,
because a shift that a person cannot reliably reach is a rostering fault as much
as a personal one.

## 3. Leave entitlement

Annual leave is eighteen working days for each completed year of service, rising
to twenty-four days after five years. Leave accrues monthly and may be taken as
it accrues, subject to approval.

Casual leave is seven days a year, taken in units of not more than two
consecutive days, and is not carried forward. Sick leave is twelve days a year.
Public holidays follow the state list at each property; a member of staff
required to work a public holiday takes an alternative day within thirty days or
is paid at twice the ordinary rate at their election.

Up to nine days of annual leave may be carried into the following year and must
be taken by 30 June, after which it lapses. Leave is not paid in lieu except on
separation.

Maternity leave is twenty-six weeks on full pay for the first two children and
twelve weeks thereafter, with the right to return to the same role.
Paternity leave is fifteen days on full pay, to be taken within six months of
the birth. Adoption and commissioning mothers receive twelve weeks. Bereavement
leave is five days on full pay for an immediate family member.

## 4. Uniform, grooming and personal presentation

Uniform is provided and laundered by the property. Two sets are issued on
joining and are replaced on fair wear. A member of staff is never required to
pay for a replacement other than where the item is lost or damaged wilfully.

Uniform is worn complete and only on duty, and is not worn to and from the
property. Changing facilities and lockers are provided for that purpose.

Grooming standards are applied to the standard of neatness rather than to
appearance. Religious dress, religious symbols, and unshorn hair or beard
maintained for religious observance are accommodated without requiring
permission to be sought. Where a safety requirement conflicts — hair covering in
the kitchen, footwear in engineering — the safety requirement is met in the way
that least disturbs the observance, and the department head arranges it.

Name badges are worn by every member of staff on duty, showing the first name
only. A member of staff who does not wish to display their name uses a preferred
name of their choosing.

## 5. Staff facilities, meals and accommodation

A meal is provided on every shift at no charge, in the staff canteen, and the
canteen menu is set with the same care as a guest menu. Staff do not eat in
guest areas and do not consume guest food, in accordance with the F&B waste
standard.

Lockers are provided to every member of staff with a key retained by the holder.
A locker is opened without the holder present only where there is a reasonable
suspicion of a criminal offence, in the presence of two managers and a colleague
nominated by the holder, and the opening is recorded.

Staff accommodation, where provided, is inspected quarterly for safety and
maintained to the same standard as guest areas for water, electricity, fire
detection and means of escape. A fault reported in staff accommodation carries
the same response target as a guest room fault.

Transport is provided for shifts starting before 06:00 or ending after 22:00.
No member of staff, and in particular no woman, is required to make their own
way to or from the property during those hours.

## 6. Grievance procedure

A grievance is raised with the department head in the first instance, in writing
or in conversation. Where the grievance concerns the department head, it is
raised with the human resources manager or the general manager directly, and the
person raising it is never required to approach the subject of the grievance
first.

The department head responds within five working days. Where the matter is not
resolved, it is escalated to the general manager, who responds within a further
ten working days. The person raising the grievance may be accompanied by a
colleague of their choosing at any meeting.

Where the matter remains unresolved, it is referred to the regional human
resources head, whose decision concludes the internal process.

Retaliation against a person for raising a grievance in good faith is gross
misconduct. This holds whether or not the grievance is upheld, and it is stated
in the outcome letter every time.

## 7. Disciplinary process

Minor matters are addressed in conversation and recorded as a note to file. The
formal process begins with a written warning, proceeds to a final written
warning, and concludes with dismissal. Warnings lapse after twelve months.

No disciplinary decision is taken without a meeting at which the person has been
told the allegation in writing at least forty-eight hours in advance, has seen
the evidence, and may be accompanied by a colleague. A meeting held without
those conditions produces no valid outcome, however clear the facts appear.

Gross misconduct may result in dismissal without notice and includes theft,
violence, falsification of records, being under the influence of alcohol or
drugs on duty, wilful breach of a safety procedure, harassment, and disclosure
of guest information.

Suspension pending investigation is on full pay, is not a disciplinary sanction,
and is stated as such in writing. An investigation is concluded within fourteen
days, and where it cannot be, the person is told why and given a new date.

A right of appeal exists against every formal sanction, heard by a manager not
previously involved, within ten working days.

## 8. Harassment and the prevention of sexual harassment

Harassment of any kind is gross misconduct. This includes conduct by a guest
towards a member of staff, and a guest who harasses a member of staff is asked
to leave the property; no member of staff is expected to tolerate it in the
interest of service, and any manager who suggests otherwise commits a
disciplinary offence themselves.

An Internal Committee is constituted at each property under the Sexual
Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act,
2013, chaired by a senior woman employee, with at least half its members women
and one external member from a non-governmental organisation familiar with the
issue.

A complaint may be made to any member of the Internal Committee within three
months of the incident, extendable at the Committee's discretion. The inquiry is
concluded within ninety days and the employer acts on the recommendation within
sixty days of receiving the report.

The identity of the complainant, the respondent, the witnesses and the contents
of the proceedings are confidential and are not published in any form. The
annual report of the number of complaints received and disposed of is filed with
the district officer.

Interim relief — a transfer, leave of up to three months on full pay, or
restraint on the respondent — is available to the complainant on request during
the inquiry and does not require the complaint to be proven first.

## 9. Training and certification

Every new member of staff completes induction before their first unsupervised
shift, covering fire and evacuation, first aid awareness, food safety awareness,
guest data handling, the harassment policy, and the layout of the property
including the location of the first aid boxes and the defibrillator.

Role-specific certification is recorded in the training register with the date
of expiry, and a member of staff whose certification has expired does not
perform the task. This applies to food handlers, first aiders, fire wardens,
lift release, electrical work and pool operation.

Refresher training is scheduled at the interval set for each certification and
is arranged before expiry rather than after. The training register is reviewed
monthly by the human resources manager and expiring certifications are reported
to the department head sixty days ahead.

Training time is working time and is paid. Training arranged outside a person's
rostered hours is compensated with time off in lieu.

## 10. Probation, appraisal and confirmation

Probation is six months for all roles and may be extended once by three months
where the reason is recorded and discussed. Confirmation is not automatic on the
passage of time and is not withheld without a conversation having taken place
first.

A review is held at the end of the second and fifth month of probation. Where
performance is not meeting the standard, this is said at the second month
rather than saved for the sixth, together with what specifically must change.

Appraisal is annual, in April, against objectives set the previous April.
Objectives are recorded in writing and are not changed retrospectively. An
appraisal rating is discussed with the person before it is finalised.

Pay is reviewed annually with effect from 1 July. A review outcome is
communicated in writing with the reasoning, whatever the outcome.

## 11. Notice, separation and exit

Notice is one month during probation and two months after confirmation, on
either side. Notice may be waived or shortened by agreement, and pay in lieu of
notice may be offered by either party.

Accrued and untaken annual leave is paid on separation. Statutory gratuity is
paid where the qualifying service is met. The final settlement is paid within
forty-five days of the last working day and is not withheld pending the return
of property, which is pursued separately.

An exit conversation is offered to every leaver and is held by the human
resources manager rather than the department head. What is said is reported in
aggregate and never attributed, and the themes are reviewed with the general
manager quarterly.

A reference is provided on request confirming the role, the dates and the reason
for leaving. We do not provide an unfavourable reference; where we would not
re-employ, we confirm the facts only.

## 12. Guest data and confidentiality

Guest information is used for the purpose of providing the stay and for no other
purpose. A member of staff accesses a guest record only where their role
requires it for the task in front of them, and every access is logged.

Guest names, room numbers, arrival and departure dates, and the fact of a
person's presence at the property are not disclosed to anyone, including a
person claiming to be a relative, an employer, or a police officer without a
written request. Callers asking to be connected to a room are connected without
confirming that the guest is resident.

Photographs of guests are not taken and images are not published on any channel,
including a personal social media account, whatever the apparent consent in the
moment.

A member of staff who becomes aware of a disclosure, a loss of a device, or an
unauthorised access reports it to the general manager the same day. Reporting
promptly is never itself treated as a disciplinary matter; concealing it is.
