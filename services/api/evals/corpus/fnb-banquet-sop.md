# Food, Beverage and Banquet Operations

Sanchari Hotels — all properties. Effective 1 April 2026.

## 1. Outlet hours and last orders

The all-day dining room serves from 06:30 to 23:00. Breakfast runs to 10:30 on
weekdays and to 11:00 on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. Last orders in
the dining room are taken at 22:30 and the room closes when the last table
leaves rather than at a fixed time.

The bar operates from 11:00 to 23:30 subject to the excise conditions in section
7. The pool deck serves from 08:00 to 19:00 and closes at sunset where that
falls earlier. The coffee lounge operates from 07:00 to 22:00.

A guest arriving within fifteen minutes of last orders is served a full menu. A
guest arriving after last orders is offered the room service menu and the
position is explained rather than the door being closed. Outlet hours are
published in the room directory, on the property website and at the entrance to
each outlet, and any temporary change is displayed at all three at least
twenty-four hours in advance.

## 2. Buffet setup, holding and replenishment

The breakfast buffet is set and temperature-checked thirty minutes before
service opens. Hot items are held above 63°C and cold items below 5°C, verified
by probe at setup and every hour thereafter, and the readings are recorded on
the service log with the name of the person who took them.

An item that has fallen out of temperature is withdrawn and discarded. It is
never reheated to bring it back into range and never moved to a different
service. Where a hot item has been below 63°C for less than two hours it may be
reheated once to above 75°C and returned to service; beyond two hours it is
discarded regardless of appearance.

No item remains on the buffet for more than four hours from the moment it is
placed. Replenishment is by replacement of the dish, not by topping up an
existing one, because topping up conceals the age of what is underneath.

Serving utensils are changed every two hours and immediately if handled by a
guest or dropped. Sneeze guards are checked at setup and cleaned between
services.

## 3. Allergen declaration and dietary requests

Every dish on every menu carries a written allergen declaration covering
cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk,
tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin and molluscs. The
declaration is held by the outlet manager and is available to a guest on
request without their having to explain why they want it.

A guest declaring an allergy is served from a dish prepared separately, not from
a portion of the general service, and the order is carried to the kitchen by the
outlet manager rather than through the ordinary ticket. The person who takes the
order tells the guest who prepared the dish when it is served.

Staff never say that a dish is free of an allergen on their own judgement. Where
the declaration does not answer the question, the chef on duty is asked and
answers directly to the guest. "It should be fine" is not an acceptable answer
and staff are told so in induction.

Vegetarian and Jain preparation is kept physically separate throughout, with
separate boards, utensils and oil, and vegetarian dishes are never carried on a
tray alongside meat.

## 4. Room service

Room service operates from 06:00 to 23:00, with a reduced overnight menu of
sandwiches, soup and beverages from 23:00 to 06:00. Orders are repeated back at
the point of taking and a delivery time is given.

The target is thirty minutes for the standard menu and forty-five minutes for
grilled items. Where an order will exceed the time given, the guest is
telephoned before the target passes rather than after it, and the reason is
given.

A tray is never left in a corridor. Trays are collected on request and by a
sweep of every floor at 11:00, 15:00 and 22:00. A tray found in a corridor is
removed immediately by whoever finds it, in any department.

A delivery charge of one hundred and fifty rupees applies per order and is
stated on the menu and at the point of ordering. There is no charge for the
overnight menu between 23:00 and 06:00.

## 5. Banquet event orders

Every event is documented in a banquet event order issued no later than seven
days before the event and circulated to kitchen, service, engineering,
housekeeping and the front desk. The order records the timings, the guaranteed
number, the menu, the beverage arrangement, the room layout, the audio-visual
requirement and the billing instruction.

A change requested inside seven days is accepted where it can be met and is
issued as a numbered revision circulated to the same list. A verbal change is
never actioned; the person receiving it issues the revision or declines it.

The guaranteed number is confirmed by the client in writing seventy-two hours
before the event and is the number that is charged, whether or not that number
attends. We prepare for five per cent above the guaranteed number, and where
attendance exceeds the guarantee the additional covers are charged at the
contracted per-head rate.

The banquet manager walks the room with the client one hour before the event
starts, against the event order rather than from memory.

## 6. Banquet cancellation and postponement

An event withdrawn more than sixty days before the date is charged nothing
beyond the deposit already paid, which is retained. Between sixty and thirty
days, twenty-five per cent of the contracted value is payable. Between thirty
and fifteen days, fifty per cent. Between fifteen and seven days, seventy-five
per cent. Inside seven days, the full contracted value is payable.

The contracted value for this purpose is the guaranteed minimum spend recorded
on the contract, not the anticipated final bill, and it excludes any charge for
services we would not have incurred.

An event moved to another date within twelve months, on a date we can
accommodate, is treated as a postponement rather than a withdrawal and no
cancellation charge arises. A postponement may be exercised once. A second
change is treated as a withdrawal at the scale that applies on the date the
second change is notified.

Where we cancel — for any reason other than an act of God, civil disturbance or
government order — we refund all sums paid, arrange a comparable venue at our
cost where one is available, and pay the difference in price.

## 7. Bar operations and licensing

The bar operates under the excise licence displayed behind the counter. Service
hours are those stated on the licence, which prevail over the hours in section 1
where they differ and which change on dry days notified by the excise
authorities.

No alcohol is served to a person under the legal drinking age for the state in
which the property operates, which is twenty-one in Kerala and twenty-one in
Karnataka. Identity is requested from any person who appears to be under
twenty-five, and the refusal of an unproven age is not negotiable regardless of
the guest's status or the manager's discretion.

Service is refused to a guest who is visibly intoxicated. The refusal is made
by the duty manager, quietly and away from other guests, and the guest is
offered food, water and assistance to their room or to transport. A refusal is
recorded with the time and the circumstances.

Stock is counted daily at close and reconciled against sales. A variance beyond
one per cent by value is reported to the F&B manager the following morning.
Bottles are never removed from the bar except against a documented transfer.

## 8. Corkage and outside food

Alcohol brought onto the property by a guest may be consumed in a guest room
without charge. It may not be consumed in any public area or outlet.

Alcohol brought to a banquet event is subject to corkage at seven hundred and
fifty rupees per bottle of wine or spirits and two hundred rupees per bottle of
beer, agreed in the contract before the event. Corkage is charged on bottles
opened, not on bottles delivered.

Food prepared outside the property is not served in any outlet or banquet space,
with two exceptions: a celebration cake, which is subject to a cutting charge of
five hundred rupees, and food prepared for religious observance, which is
accommodated without charge and handled separately from our own preparation.

We accept no responsibility for the condition of food or beverage we did not
prepare, and this is stated in the banquet contract rather than raised
afterwards.

## 9. Food safety, temperature and traceability

Deliveries are temperature-checked at the point of receipt. Chilled goods are
accepted below 5°C and frozen goods below minus 18°C. A delivery outside range
is refused in whole, not in part, and the refusal is recorded against the
supplier.

Cooked food is held above 63°C or chilled to below 5°C within ninety minutes of
cooking. Reheated food reaches 75°C throughout. Probes are cleaned and
calibrated weekly against iced water and boiling water, and the calibration is
recorded.

Every batch cooked for a banquet or a buffet is sampled at two hundred grams,
labelled with the date, the item and the service, and held at minus 18°C for
seventy-two hours. Samples are discarded only after the seventy-two hours have
elapsed with no report.

Stock rotates first in, first out, and every stored item carries a date label
applied at the point of opening or preparation. An item without a legible date
label is discarded rather than judged by appearance.

## 10. Kitchen hygiene and audit

The kitchen is cleaned to the published schedule at the end of every service,
and the schedule is signed by the chef on duty. Extraction canopies and ducting
are cleaned by contract quarterly and the certificate is held by the F&B
manager.

An internal hygiene audit is carried out monthly by the F&B manager and the
executive chef jointly, scored against the published checklist. A score below
eighty-five per cent triggers a re-audit within fourteen days. Two consecutive
scores below eighty-five per cent are reported to the general manager.

Staff handling food undergo a medical examination on joining and annually
thereafter. A member of staff with diarrhoea, vomiting, jaundice, a septic skin
lesion or a discharging ear, eye or nose does not handle food and does not enter
the kitchen, and reports the condition rather than waiting to be asked.

Hand washing stations are stocked at all times and are never used for washing
equipment or food.

## 11. Waste, leftovers and donation

Food that has been on service is not offered to staff, is not taken home, and is
not donated. This is not a matter of generosity but of traceability: once food
has left our temperature control we cannot stand behind it.

Food prepared and never placed on service, held within temperature throughout,
may be donated to a nominated charity against a signed transfer recording the
time, the temperature at transfer and the quantity. Donation is arranged by the
F&B manager and never by an individual.

Wet waste is segregated at source and collected twice daily. Used cooking oil is
sold only to an authorised collector against a receipt, and never disposed of to
drain. Dry recyclable waste is segregated and weighed monthly.

Waste by weight is recorded per service and reviewed monthly against covers
served. A rising ratio is treated as a production forecasting problem before it
is treated as a wastage problem.

## 12. Supplier deliveries and receiving

Deliveries are received at the service entrance between 06:00 and 11:00 only.
A delivery outside those hours is accepted only with the prior agreement of the
F&B manager, because a delivery received without a trained receiver is a
delivery nobody checked.

Every delivery is checked against the purchase order for quantity, quality,
temperature and date coding before the delivery note is signed. Signing for
goods not checked is a disciplinary matter, since the signature is the only
evidence we have if the goods are wrong.

Vehicles are inspected for cleanliness and for the segregation of raw and
ready-to-eat goods. A vehicle carrying raw meat unsegregated from ready-to-eat
product is refused and the refusal is reported to the supplier in writing the
same day.

Short deliveries and rejections are recorded on the delivery note before it is
signed, photographed, and notified to purchasing the same day so that the credit
is raised in the month it arises.
